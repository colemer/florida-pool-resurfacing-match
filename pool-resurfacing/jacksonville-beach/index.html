Pool resurfacing in Jacksonville Beach: what's different here

Jacksonville Beach pools sit on a barrier island with storm-surge history, and the city runs its own deep-well water. Ask the city for hardness before a new finish is filled.

  • Fill water: Jacksonville Beach supplies its own water from artesian wells 900 to 1,200 feet into the Floridan Aquifer; its 2025 report describes five wells and aeration at the plant.[4][5]
  • Pool permits in Jacksonville Beach go through City of Jacksonville Beach Building Inspection Division; ask whether your resurfacing or equipment job needs one.[6]

Recent storms in this area

Storms bring debris, power outages and runoff into pools; these are the recent events recorded for this area.

  • Hurricane Matthew (October 7, 2016): The Jacksonville Beach Pier reported 61 mph winds with gusts to 76 mph, and the Mayport tide gauge nearby reached 5.14 feet NAVD88, the second-highest level since the 1898 hurricane.[1]
  • Hurricane Irma (September 11, 2017): A storm tide sensor at Jacksonville Beach recorded a water level of 6.55 feet NAVD88 (4.1 feet above MHHW), and surge rushed through dune gaps left by Matthew.[2][3]

Who issues permits in Jacksonville Beach?

City of Jacksonville Beach Building Inspection Division. Phone: 904-247-6235. Jacksonville Beach issues its own permits (online E-Permit or in person at 11 N. 3rd St.). It is not part of the consolidated City of Jacksonville.[6]

Florida defines interior refinishing, equipment replacement and draining a pool for repair as pool/spa contractor work. Ask the office whether your resurfacing or equipment job needs a permit before work starts.

What to look for in a Jacksonville Beach pool contractor

Four checks before you sign anything. How our matching works.

Check 1 · License

Check the pool contractor license

  • Interior refinishing and substantial draining for repair fall within Florida's pool/spa contractor scope
  • Look the license number up on DBPR's free Verify a License search
  • Ask whether your city requires a permit for the job

Check 2 · Insurance

Proof of insurance

  • Ask for a current certificate of general liability insurance in the company's name
  • Ask whether the crew working at your home is covered by workers' compensation
  • Call the insurer on the certificate to confirm the policy is active

Check 3 · Estimate

A written, itemized estimate

  • Scope, materials and quantities in writing, not just a total
  • Who applies for any permit, and who pays the permit fee
